The numbers under the tokens are illustrative IDs, from GPT-4’s tokenizer. IDs depend on the model’s tokenizer, so other models can number them differently.

Illustrative values. A real model’s list has thousands of numbers, learned in training, and they work like map coordinates.

4Step 4: The numbers land in a map of meaning

Start the sentence:

Moves bank on the map of meaning

A 2-D sketch, drawn by hand: the real space has thousands of dimensions.

The token “bank” always starts with the same list of numbers. Then the model’s later layers mix in the words before it, and those numbers shift.

    Count the R's

    How many R's are in "strawberry"? Some AI chatbots have said 2.

    AI doesn't read letters. It reads tokens, chunks of words like "str" + "aw" + "berry," so it never really sees each R. Know how it reads and you'll know where it slips.

    Which word do you think would trip it up next?

    A video version of the text under “What’s on screen.”

    What’s on screen
    1. "How many R's are in strawberry?" Letter tiles spell S-T-R-A-W-B-E-R-R-Y. "Count them in your head."
    2. "We asked an AI." Student: "How many R's are in 'strawberry'?" AI: "There are 2 R's in 'strawberry.'"
    3. "Let's count." The three R tiles light up, numbered 1, 2, 3. Stamp: "It's 3."
    4. "AI doesn't see letters. It sees tokens." The tiles split into three tokens: STR, AW, BERRY. "Tokens are chunks of words, like str + aw + berry. It never really sees each R."
    5. "Know how it reads. Spot where it slips." Merc: "Want to find its other blind spots?"

    How AI works

    No Such Book

    Your AI found the perfect source. The book doesn't exist.

    AI can invent citations with a real-sounding author, title, publisher, and page number. Before you cite anything, search the exact title, check a library catalog, or look on the publisher's website.

    A video version of the text under “What’s on screen.”

    What’s on screen
    1. "Your AI found you the perfect source." AI: "Here's a perfect source for your essay:" Citation: "Marsh, Helen. (2019). The Secret Life of Pencils. Brightwater Press, p. 112."
    2. "Let's go find it." The library catalog search for "The Secret Life of Pencils" returns 0 found.
    3. "The book doesn't exist. The AI made it up." Stamp: "Not found." "No author. No publisher. No page 112."
    4. "AI can invent sources that sound totally real." A book: real-sounding title and author. A link: goes nowhere. A quote: with a page number. Each is stamped "Made up."
    5. "Look it up before you cite it." Merc: "Where could we check if a book is real?" Library catalog. Publisher's website. Search the exact title.
    6. End card: Merc. "Mercurius. Look it up first." Download on the App Store. Search "Mercurius AI" on iPhone.

    Stay safe

    The Call

    Your mom calls. She sounds scared and needs money right now. It isn't her.

    AI can copy a voice from a few seconds of audio, like a birthday video posted online. If a call feels urgent and off, hang up and call back on a number you know. Better yet, pick a family code word today.

    A video version of the text under “What’s on screen.”

    What’s on screen
    1. "Your mom calls. She sounds scared." An incoming call from "Mom."
    2. "She needs money. Right now." On the call: "Honey, it's me." "I lost my wallet and my phone." "Please send $300 right now." "Please hurry."
    3. "It wasn't her. It was an AI voice." The caller is now "Unknown voice." Stamp: "Fake."
    4. "AI can copy a voice from a short video." Mom's 4-second post, "Happy birthday to my favorite kid!", turns into an "AI voice copy."
    5. "Hang up. Call back. Or use a code word." Merc: "What's a question only your real family could answer?" Student: "What's our dog's middle name?"
    6. End card: Merc. "Mercurius. Hang up. Call back." Download on the App Store. Search "Mercurius AI" on iPhone.
